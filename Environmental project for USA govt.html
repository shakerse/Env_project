      Introduction

      The 2022 National Lakes Assessment (NLA) is a national survey run by the U.S. EPA to evaluate the condition of lakes across the United States. In this report I use the 2022 site log, algal toxin results and water-chemistry results to see:

      how many states, lakes and samples were included,

      summarise algal toxin occurrence by state,

      and explore relationships between algal toxins and key nutrient and water-quality variables.

      Data Analysis

      Toxin Summary Table (Per State × Analyte)

      tox_summary <- nla_2022 %>%
  filter(analyte %in% c("MICX", "CYLSPER")) %>%
  mutate(detected = conc > mdl) %>%
  group_by(state, analyte) %>%
  summarise(
    n_samples   = n_distinct(uid),
    n_detect    = n_distinct(uid[detected]),
    pct_detect  = round(100 * n_detect / n_samples, 1),
    min_conc    = ifelse(any(detected), min(conc[detected], na.rm = TRUE), NA_real_),
    mean_conc   = ifelse(any(detected), mean(conc[detected], na.rm = TRUE), NA_real_),
    sd_conc     = ifelse(any(detected), sd(conc[detected], na.rm = TRUE), NA_real_),
    max_conc    = ifelse(any(detected), max(conc[detected], na.rm = TRUE), NA_real_),
    .groups = "drop"
  )

      Conclusion

      Across all sampled lakes, microcystins (MICX) were detected more frequently than cylindrospermopsin (CYLSPER). Detection frequencies and concentration ranges varied among states, but in general MICX detections were common while CYLSPER was relatively rare.

      Lakes classified as man-made and those with larger surface areas were well represented in the NLA 2022 design. Scatterplots relating toxin concentrations to nutrients and chlorophyll-a suggest that higher toxin concentrations tend to occur in nutrient-enriched systems, although there is substantial scatter indicating the influence of additional environmental drivers.
